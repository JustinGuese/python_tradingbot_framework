# Earnings drift (PEAD) sleeve — negative result, 2026-09-22

**Not built.** In 93 US large caps, buying after an EPS beat does not beat simply
holding the same stocks.

## Method

`scripts/onetime_pead_backtest.py`:
- **Universe:** 93 of the largest US stocks.
- **Events:** 8,524 earnings events from yfinance `get_earnings_dates`, 2014 → 2026.
- **Entry:** after an EPS surprise ≥ the threshold, go long at the close of the
  first session that fully follows the announcement. That is the same day for a
  pre-open release, otherwise the next session, so there is no look-ahead.
- **Portfolio:** hold for N sessions, keep at most K names (largest surprise
  first), equal weight, rest in cash. Costs: 5 bps per unit of turnover.

It is scored two ways:
- **vs QQQ,** the fleet's target metric.
- **vs an equal-weight hold of the same 93 stocks.** Today's mega caps are
  survivors, so the universe alone looks like alpha. Only this second
  comparison isolates the earnings-timing edge.

## Results (2016 → 2026)

Each cell shows alpha, then the t-stat in brackets. The last column is the edge
this sleeve would need to have.

| Hold / max names / min surprise | vs QQQ, full | vs same universe, H1 2016–20 | vs same universe, H2 2021–26 | vs same universe, full |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 60 / 10 / 5% | +5.9% (1.72) | +2.9% (0.74) | +0.9% (0.23) | +1.9% (0.69) |
| 20 / 10 / 5% | +3.0% (0.87) | −3.4% (−0.80) | +3.3% (0.77) | +0.2% (0.05) |
| 60 / 10 / 10% | +5.1% (1.46) | +2.3% (0.61) | −0.4% (−0.09) | +1.0% (0.36) |
| 40 / 5 / 10% | +1.0% (0.22) | +0.6% (0.10) | −6.0% (−1.02) | −2.8% (−0.71) |

Holding the universe equal-weight scores +4.7% vs QQQ (t 1.81) with no signal at
all. Most of the first column is that survivor effect.

## Why

Earnings drift is one of the best-documented anomalies, but it lives mostly in
small, thinly covered stocks. In mega caps, dozens of analysts price the surprise
within a day. This matches the post-2000 literature on the anomaly decaying.

## What was fixed along the way

The **insider** half of EarningsInsiderTiltBot never worked:
- yfinance leaves the `Transaction` column blank, and the loader stored it
  verbatim, so all 2,684 insider rows had an empty type. The score only counts
  rows typed "Purchase" or "Sale", so it was 0 on every run.
- The loader now reads the direction from `Text` ("Sale at price …"), and it
  types legacy rows in place instead of inserting duplicates.
  See `_transaction_type_from_text` in `utils/stock_fundamentals_loader.py`.

Two caveats remain. yfinance returns only about 6 months of insider history, so
this signal cannot be backtested. And in these mega caps nearly every insider row
is a routine sale, while the documented insider effect comes from purchases,
which are rare here. Expect little from it even now that it works.
